1 · Problem and users

Empty slots, longer waits, reminders spread evenly

20.2%of 110,527 appointments were missed without notice
  1. The problem. Unannounced no-shows waste clinic capacity and lengthen waiting times for other patients.
  2. What is missing. Staff cannot tell which appointments most need a reminder.
  3. Who uses it. Operations and scheduling staff in a private outpatient clinic network.
  4. The rule. Decision support only: a person always decides; the system never cancels or rebooks.
2 · Data

Booking lead time is the strongest signal

Kaggle Medical Appointment No Shows: 110,527 appointments, 62,299 patients, public clinics in Vitória, Brazil (6 weeks in 2016).

A trap in the data

Raw, patients who got an SMS reminder missed more appointments (27.6% vs 16.7%). SMS was only sent for bookings made 3+ days ahead. Compared at the same lead time, SMS goes with fewer no-shows. SMS status is also unknown at booking time, so it is excluded.

3 · Method

Only what is known when the appointment is booked

  1. 1Booked appointment

    Age, gender, neighbourhood, welfare status, health flags, booking and appointment dates.

  2. 2Leakage-safe features

    Lead time, a same-day flag, and past attendance counted only before the booking day.

  3. 3Logistic regression

    Chosen from 23 logged validation runs; committed before the test set was used.

    6 · Honest findings

    What did not go to plan, reported as it happened

    Gradient boosting won on the test week

    +0.013 ROC-AUC (95% CI +0.009 to +0.018), better in 100% of bootstrap resamples, although on validation the gap was only noise.

    Logistic regression was kept, as pre-registered: switching after seeing test scores would make the test result a biased estimate. Gradient boosting is recommended for the next version, confirmed on new data.

    Success criteria, set before modelling

    • PartlyBeat both baselines: yes vs majority and lead-time rule; no model reliably beat logistic regression
    • Not metBeat logistic regression's top-20% recall: same reason
    • MetNo leakage, verified by automated tests

    7 · Error analysis and fairness

    Where the model fails, and for whom

    Caught no-shows and false alarms look almost identical in the data (young, booked weeks ahead), which limits any model. Within a single lead-time group, ranking is only a little better than chance (ROC-AUC 0.57–0.62).

    9 · Responsible use and limitations

    Helpful for reminders, never for decisions about care

    Intended use

    • Staff get a ranked list and choose whom to remind or call
    • Supportive help only: reminders, confirmation calls, rescheduling offers
    • A person always decides; the model never acts

    Never use it to

    • Cancel, delay, or refuse care: 2 in 3 flagged patients would have come
    • Overbook flagged patients' slots or penalise patients
    • Treat the probability as an exact chance

    Known limitation

    Moderate accuracy (ROC-AUC 0.72) from six weeks of 2016 data at one Brazilian city's public clinics; linear age effect; probabilities about 2.7 points too high on the test week.
