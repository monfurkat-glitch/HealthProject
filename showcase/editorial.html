Abstract

In the data, one in five outpatient appointments is missed without notice. This project trains a model that estimates each appointment's no-show risk at booking time, so clinic staff can focus reminders and follow-up calls on the appointments most likely to be missed. On a held-out test week, the pre-registered logistic regression reached ROC-AUC 0.723, and its High-risk band (23% of appointments) contained 41% of all no-shows. The tool is decision support for supportive reminders only.

1Problem and users

Unannounced no-shows waste clinic capacity and lengthen waiting times for other patients. Without a risk estimate, staff cannot tell which appointments most need a reminder, so reminders are spread evenly.

Users: operations and scheduling staff in a private outpatient clinic network, who decide whom to remind or call. The model is decision support only; a person always decides.

2Method

Only information known when the appointment is booked is used. SMS reminder status is excluded, because reminders are sent after booking.

  1. Booked appointmentAge, gender, neighbourhood, welfare status, health flags, and booking and appointment dates.
  2. Leakage-safe featuresLead time, a same-day flag, and past attendance counted only before the booking day.
  3. Logistic regressionChosen from 23 logged validation runs; the choice was committed before the test set was used.
  4. Risk band for staffA probability, a Low / Medium / High band, and the main factors; staff decide whom to contact.

3Results

Final results come from a held-out test week (17,676 appointments, 2016-06-03 to 2016-06-08) that was used only once.

What this shows

  • Beats both baselines: 0.723 vs 0.692 (lead-time rule) and 0.500 (majority class).
  • Generalises as expected: the test score matches validation (0.726); leakage is checked by automated tests.
  • Separates risk clearly: High-band appointments are missed 4 times as often as Low-band ones (33.2% vs 8.1%).
  • Honest model choice: gradient boosting scored higher on the test week (0.737); the pre-registered choice was kept and gradient boosting is recommended for a future version.

6Responsible use and limitations

Intended use

  • Staff get a ranked list and choose whom to remind or call
  • Supportive help only: reminders, confirmation calls, rescheduling offers
  • A person always decides; the model never acts

Never use it to

  • Cancel, delay, or refuse care: 2 in 3 flagged patients would have come
  • Overbook flagged patients' slots or penalise patients
  • Treat the probability as an exact chance

Known limitation

Moderate accuracy (ROC-AUC 0.72) from six weeks of 2016 data at one Brazilian city's public clinics. Welfare patients and children receive more false alarms and elderly no-shows are rarely flagged, so the tool is only for supportive reminders, never to cancel, delay, or refuse care.
