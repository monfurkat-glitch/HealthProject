01 · Context

The project in one clear story.

In the data, one in five outpatient appointments is missed without notice. The project gives staff an earlier, ranked signal instead of spreading reminders evenly.

The problem

Unannounced no-shows waste clinic capacity and lengthen waiting times for other patients. Without a risk estimate, staff cannot tell which appointments most need a reminder.

Who uses it

Operations and scheduling staff in a private outpatient clinic network, who decide whom to remind or call.

Decision support onlyA person always decides
02 · System

How it works, without the notebook noise.

Only information known when the appointment is booked is used. SMS reminder status is excluded, because reminders are sent after booking.

1

Booked appointment

Age, gender, neighbourhood, welfare status, health flags, and booking and appointment dates.

2

Leakage-safe features

Lead time, a same-day flag, and past attendance counted only before the booking day.

3

Logistic regression

Chosen from 23 logged validation runs; the choice was committed before the test set was used.

4

Risk band for staff

A probability, a Low / Medium / High band, and the main factors; staff decide whom to contact.

What this proves

  • Beats both baselines0.723 vs 0.692 (lead-time rule) and 0.500 (majority class) test ROC-AUC.
  • Generalises as expectedThe test score matches validation (0.726); leakage is checked by automated tests.
  • Separates risk clearlyHigh-band appointments are missed 4 times as often as Low-band ones (33.2% vs 8.1%).
  • Honest model choiceGradient boosting scored higher on the test week (0.737); the pre-registered choice was kept and gradient boosting is recommended for a future version.

05 · Responsible use

Helpful for reminders, not for decisions about care.

Intended use

  • Staff get a ranked list and choose whom to remind or call
  • Supportive help only: reminders, confirmation calls, rescheduling offers
  • A person always decides; the model never acts

Never use it to

  • Cancel, delay, or refuse care: 2 in 3 flagged patients would have come
  • Overbook flagged patients' slots or penalise patients
  • Treat the probability as an exact chance

Known limitation

Moderate accuracy (ROC-AUC 0.72) from six weeks of 2016 data at one Brazilian city's public clinics. Welfare patients and children receive more false alarms and elderly no-shows are rarely flagged, so the tool is only for supportive reminders, never to cancel, delay, or refuse care.
